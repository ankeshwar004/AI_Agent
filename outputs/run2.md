## Snapshot
Q1 FY27 revenue rose 18% YoY to ₹1,248 crore, with profit after tax of ₹82 crore and EBITDA margin slipping to 11.2% (down 170 bps). [S3]
Order book grew to ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026, up from ₹3,150 crore a year earlier. [S3]
The new Bharuch plant, adding about 30% to cable capacity, is slated for commercial production in Q3 FY27. [S3]
Promoter pledging fell to 4.1% of promoter holdings as of June 2026, down from 6% in March 2026. [S8]

## Bull case
- Revenue growth is driven by strong demand from power utilities and data‑centre projects, with data‑centre cables now 9% of domestic revenue and targeted to double in two years. [S2]
- The Bharuch expansion adds 30% capacity, positioning the company to capture higher‑margin segments as demand rises. [S3]
- Net debt‑to‑equity stands at 0.35, indicating a low leverage profile and financial flexibility for future investments. [S2]

## Bear case
- Copper price surge (≈14%) compressed operating margins by 170 bps, reducing EBITDA margin to 11.2%. [S1] [S3]
- Receivable days increased to 96 days, raising cash‑flow collection risk. [S2]
- A GST demand order of ₹46.3 crore for FY2020‑21/21‑22 could materialise if the appeal fails, posing a contingent liability. [S7]
- Earlier reports flagged promoter pledging at 35% (2024), highlighting governance concerns despite more recent lower pledging levels. [S4] [S8]

## Open questions
- Whether the company can successfully pass on higher copper costs to customers over the next two quarters remains uncertain. [S3]
- Exact commercial start‑up timeline and ramp‑up performance of the Bharuch plant are not yet verified. [S3]
- Potential impact on liquidity from the rising receivable days and any future defaults is unclear. [S2]
- Outcome of the GST demand order appeal and any associated penalties or interest are pending. [S7]
