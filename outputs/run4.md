## Snapshot
Q1 FY27 revenue rose 18% YoY to ₹1,248 crore, with profit after tax increasing 6% to ₹82 crore [S3]
EBITDA margin fell 170 basis points to 11.2% as higher copper prices compressed margins [S3] [S1]
Order book grew to ₹3,900 crore, up from ₹3,150 crore a year earlier, and exports now account for 21% of revenue [S3]
Receivable days increased to 96 days from 78 days, driven by two large state utility customers [S2]

## Bull case
- Strong demand from power transmission and data‑centre projects is driving revenue growth, with data‑centre cables now 9% of domestic sales and expected to double in two years [S2]
- The new Bharuch plant will add about 30% to cable capacity and is slated for commercial production in Q3 FY27, supporting future volume expansion [S3]
- Export share rose to 21% of revenue, providing geographic diversification [S3]
- Promoter pledge is low at 4.1% of promoter holdings as of June 2026, reducing governance risk [S8]

## Bear case
- Margins are under pressure: EBITDA margin declined 170 bps to 11.2% due to a 14% rise in copper prices during the quarter [S1] [S3]
- Receivable days have risen to 96 days, indicating potential cash‑flow strain and slower payments from large utility customers [S2]
- The company faces a GST demand order of ₹46.3 crore for prior‑year input‑tax credit claims, with uncertain outcome of the appeal [S7]
- Historical promoter pledging was high (35% of promoter holdings in early 2024), raising past governance concerns and creating uncertainty about current pledge levels [S4] [S8]

## Open questions
- How quickly can Sarvottam Cables pass on higher copper costs to customers, given price‑variation clauses reset with a lag of one to two quarters [S2]
- What will be the final financial impact if the GST demand order is upheld after appeal [S7]
- Current promoter pledge level: discrepancy between 2024 news report (35% pledged) and 2026 shareholding filing (4.1% pledged) needs clarification [S4] [S8]
- Future capex plans beyond the Bharuch plant and potential debt implications, given net debt‑to‑equity of 0.35 times [S2]
